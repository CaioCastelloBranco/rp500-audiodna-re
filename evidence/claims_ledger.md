## C1 - What the RP500 actually streams to its DSP

**Old claim.** the RP500 streams coefficient blocks out of code space - two streams, bank 14 source 0x2DC2 (cmd 0xC0/0xC1), bank 15 source 0x2E3B (command 0xA0), one byte at a time to port 0xD070

**New claim.** the RP500 streams a 24-byte PARAMETER DESCRIPTOR from code space - identical in both cabinet banks and immediately followed by executable code - not an IR-sized payload

Old claim holds: **False**   New claim holds: **True**

Evidence:

- bank 14 0x2DC2..0x2DD9 : c9 12 12 34 12 10 00 09 01 01 0c 50 0a 20 01 00 12 ab 02 0a ed 02 00 1f
- bank 15 0x2E3B..0x2E52 : c9 12 12 34 12 10 00 09 01 01 0c 50 0a 20 01 00 12 ab 02 0a ed 02 00 1f
- the two blocks are byte-for-byte identical : True
- the byte after the block: bank14 0x2DDA=E6  bank15 0x2E53=E6  (0xE6 = MOV A,@R0 - the head of the next function)
- the two descriptor blocks live in DIFFERENT banks at DIFFERENT addresses yet are identical - so they are not cabinet-specific

## C2 - The 0x7FF8 byte cap

**Old claim.** the stream is capped at 0x7FF8 bytes per stream

**New claim.** there is NO length limit in the host code; the 0x7FF8 compare is a STALL ABORT on the 'the DSP status did not change' path

Old claim holds: **False**   New claim holds: **True**

Evidence:

- bank 15 0xBE29..0xBE42 (the compare is the FALL-THROUGH of the jump-if-set):
-     BE29: e5 30     MOV A,30h
-     BE2B: 65 2f     XRL A,2Fh
-     BE2D: 20 e2 1c  JB E2h,BE4C
-     BE30: a8 38     MOV R0,38h
-     BE32: 08        INC R0
-     BE33: 06        INC @R0
-     BE34: e6        MOV A,@R0
-     BE35: 18        DEC R0
-     BE36: 86 06     MOV 06h,@R0
-     BE38: 70 01     JNZ BE3B
-     BE3A: 06        INC @R0
-     BE3B: 14        DEC A
-     BE3C: c3        CLR C
-     BE3D: 94 f8     SUBB A,#F8h
-     BE3F: ee        MOV A,R6
-     BE40: 94 7f     SUBB A,#7Fh
-     BE42: 50 6c     JNC BEB0
- 0xBE2D `JB E2h,0xBE4C` = jump to the SEND path when bit 2 changed; the 0x7FF8 compare at 0xBE3D is reached only when it did NOT change
- measured with a DSP model that always acknowledges: 118650 bytes pushed (0x7FF8 = 32760) - the cap never fired

## C4 - How many mechanisms write the DSP data port 0xD070

**Old claim.** the RP500 writes the DSP data port 0xD070 exactly twice

**New claim.** three mechanisms: two code-sourced descriptor stores, plus a 113-byte configuration block copied byte-wise into DSP registers 0xD000-0xD070 (its last byte lands on 0xD070)

Old claim holds: **False**   New claim holds: **True**

Evidence:

- code-sourced stores `MOV DPTR,#0D070h ; MOVX @DPTR,A` : 2 (bank 14 0x9ABA, bank 15 0xBEAC)
- the configuration loop exists in banks [14, 15], table bases {14: '0x2c80', 15: '0x2cbb'}
- bank 15 0xB886..0xB896: 90 2C BB 93 FD E4 2F FF EE 34 D0 8F 82 F5 83 ED F0
-    90 2c bb = MOV DPTR,#2CBBh ; 93 = MOVC (read the table byte) ; 34 d0 = ADDC A,#D0h ; 8f 82/f5 83 = DPL/DPH ; f0 = MOVX @DPTR,A
-    => the table byte is written to DSP register 0xD000+index, index 0x00..0x70 (0x59 skipped) -> index 0x70 is 0xD070
- the loop bounds: bank 15 0xB872 94 71 EE 94 00 50 (compare against 0x0071) and 0xB87A EF 64 59 4E 60 (skip index 0x59)
- the table's last byte (index 0x70) = bank14 00, bank15 00
- EMULATOR PROOF: the archived uCsim log contains a stop at 0xB897 - the instruction right after that configuration write - caused by the 0xD070 write event: True

## C8 - Does the GSP1101 firmware address its two AudioDNA2 chips independently? (NEW)

**Old claim.** the GSP selects its second chip through a command byte staged in `0xFDFC` (doc §13.3)

**New claim.** the firmware does **not** address the chips independently. `0xFDFC` is RP500-only (49 refs; **0** in every GSP build v11→C63). The IR path writes one `0xD070` port and one `0xE000` flow-control byte; the only GSP-only two-select access is `0xE400` ← `0x40`/`0x80` + read `0xEC40`, a status/ID path. The transport predates user cabs: the oldest build (v11) already has it with cells `0x2164`/`0x1F5D`/`0x2B4F`, source `0x2063+index`.

Old claim holds: **False**   New claim holds: **True**

Evidence:

- `0xFDFC` refs: GSP v11/v20/C48/C63 = 0/0/0/0; RP500 = 49
- `0xD070` stores: every GSP build = 34 (2 code-space + 32 XRAM = 2 handlers × 16 banks); RP500 = 2 (2 code-space + 0 XRAM)
- both C63 handlers reference `0x2704`, `0x24FD`, `0x2705`, `0x327A`; neither references `0xE200` or `0xE400`
- `0xE400` immediate values: GSP = {0x01, 0x40, 0x80}; RP500 = none
- `0xEC40` refs: GSP = 5; RP500 = 0
- RP500 board photo: one AudioDNA2 (U25, `72-1002 GREEN`), one SDRAM (U24), one flash (U26); no second DSP footprint
- what it does **not** settle: whether the two GSP chips differ in microcode, and whether `0xD070` is broadcast or hardware-muxed to them - both require a bus capture or the DSP microcode
